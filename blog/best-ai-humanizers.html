7 best AI humanizer tools in 2026

Seven AI humanizer tools compared on price, word limits and free trials: Undetectable.ai, WriteHuman, HIX Bypass, Smodin, Walter Writes, QuillBot and Text Humanize.

Search for an AI humanizer and you will find the same eight names on every list, each one claiming to be the best. The claims rarely agree, because the tools are not competing on the same thing: some sell the lowest yearly price, some sell volume, some meter by request, and one of them is free with no catch. This is our comparison of seven of them — what each costs, what you actually get for the money, and which buyer each one suits.

Disclosure: Text Humanize is one of the seven tools below, and it is ours. We have tried to be even-handed about it, and every price was read from the vendor's own pricing page in September 2026. Prices in this category move constantly, so confirm on the vendor's site before you buy.

How we compared them

Four things decide whether a humanizer is worth your money, and they matter more than any bypass percentage a marketing page publishes:

  • Price against volume. The entry plan's monthly cost and how many words it carries. A cheap plan with 10,000 words is not cheaper than a dearer plan with 100,000 if you write every week — do the arithmetic per 1,000 words.
  • What you can test for free. A trial sized at 250 words shows you the interface; a recurring free tier shows you the quality across real documents.
  • A detector you can check with. A tool with a built-in detector lets you score the rewrite without paying for a second subscription. Better still if it names the outside detectors it claims to clear — that is a checkable claim.
  • What it is honestly for. Some of these are humanizers in the strict sense; others are writing suites with a humanize button. Neither is a bad purchase, but they solve different problems.

One warning before the table: no tool can guarantee a pass. Detectors retrain, institutions tune their own sensitivity, and the independent October 2026 cycle at HumanizerBench put the best-scoring tool at 74.6% bypass — that is the honest ceiling of this category right now, not the 99% figures some vendors advertise.

1. Undetectable.ai — best for the lowest yearly price

The most established name in the category and, billed annually, the cheapest serious option of the paid tools here: about $5 a month for 10,000 words, with a $9.99 monthly plan if you would rather not commit. Higher tiers lift the allowance to 20,000 and 35,000 words, and a business plan carries API access. It ships its own detector, offers purpose modes (essay, blog, email) and writing-level matching, and supports 50-plus languages.

The trial is one-time: 250 words, no card, after which it is paid plans only. It is also the most reviewed tool in the space, which cuts both ways — you will find plenty of independent test data, including benchmark cycles where it placed third of fourteen. We wrote a full guide on whether Undetectable.ai is legit if that is the name on your shortlist.

Best for: buyers who want the market leader at the lowest annual cost. Skip if: 10,000 words a month will not cover your output, or you want a free route instead - see our free Undetectable.ai alternative.

2. WriteHuman — best for long documents

WriteHuman meters by request instead of by word, which is the right model if you humanize occasional long pieces rather than a steady stream of short ones: 80 requests a month at up to 600 words each on the $12-a-month yearly plan, with an unlimited tier above it. The free allowance is 3 requests of 250 words per month — the most useful recurring free test of any paid tool in this comparison. It bundles a detector, a REST API and, unusually, an MCP server for agent workflows.

Best for: writers filing a few long documents, and anyone building tooling around a humanizer API. Skip if: you write many short posts — per-request metering wastes quota on those.

3. HIX Bypass — best for testing before paying

If you want to judge output quality on your own writing before any card details are involved, HIX Bypass (marketed alongside BypassGPT) gives 150 free words every month — a recurring allowance, not a one-off — against plans from roughly $8 a month billed yearly for 5,000 words. What we like about its claims is that they name the detectors it aims to clear, which makes them checkable rather than vague. Its weakness is volume: 5,000 words is a light month's work for anyone publishing regularly.

Best for: sceptical buyers who want to verify quality for free first. Skip if: you need serious volume from the entry tier.

4. Walter Writes — best for volume on a budget

At about $8 a month billed yearly, Walter Writes carries 30,000 words — the largest allowance in this table per dollar at entry level, roughly a third of what most competitors charge for that much volume. The catch is structural rather than hidden: each request on that tier is capped at 750 words, so long documents run in several passes, and the score it reports is its own in-house measure rather than a reading from an outside detector. There is a free allowance, though the vendor does not publish its size.

Best for: high monthly word counts on a small budget, if the request cap fits your documents. Skip if: you want proof against third-party detectors inside the tool.

5. Smodin — best for students and multilingual writing

Smodin is a nine-in-one academic suite — writer, detector, humanizer, plagiarism checker, rewriter, translator and more — at about $10 a month billed yearly, with a genuine free tier that needs no card. Its differentiators are citation preservation (your APA, MLA and Chicago references survive the rewrite) and 100-plus language support, the widest multilingual coverage of any tool here. Two honest caveats: the word allowance is published only as "Unlimited" on Premium with no figure attached, and the plan labels "Standard" and "Advanced" for the humanizer are not defined anywhere on the pricing page.

Best for: students and multilingual writers who want a whole toolkit in one subscription. Skip if: you want a dedicated humanizer with transparent limits.

6. QuillBot — best if you want editing, not evasion

QuillBot is the odd entry, deliberately. It is a paraphrasing and grammar suite with a humanize mode, it makes no detector claims at all, and it is the only tool in this table that never promises to clear anything — which makes it the most honest thing on the page. Premium runs $8.33 a month billed yearly with unlimited paraphrasing; the free tier allows 125 words and six humanizer uses a day. If your goal is a better sentence rather than a passing score, this is the better tool, and it pairs well with a humanizer rather than replacing one.

Best for: everyday rewriting and grammar help. Skip if: you specifically need to lower a detector score.

7. Text Humanize — best for zero budget

Our own tool, so take this section with the disclosure above in mind. Text Humanize is free and supported by advertising: no account, no subscription, no daily word quota. Each check accepts up to 10,000 characters (about 1,500 words), the AI detector is free with no sign-up, and the rewrite shows a word-by-word comparison against your original so you can keep your facts, numbers and links intact. What it does not have is an annual pricing tier, an API, or a bypass guarantee — it is a browser tool for people who write occasionally and do not want another subscription.

Best for: students and occasional writers at zero cost. Skip if: you need API access, team plans or large monthly volume.

How to choose — and how to test before you pay

Work backwards from the detector that will read your work, not from the price list. If your concern is a specific tool — Turnitin for a thesis, GPTZero for a blog — check recent independent results for that detector, since bypass rates vary more between detectors than between humanizers. Then test the shortlist on writing where you already know the answer: one paragraph you wrote yourself and one paragraph generated by your model of choice. Run each through the free trial, then through our free AI detector or the detector your institution uses.

Two rules keep this honest. First, anyone promising a permanent 100% bypass is describing a moving target they do not control — detectors produce false positives and misses constantly, and that cuts both ways. Second, read what the rewrite actually changed; the patterns detectors measure are sentence rhythm, word predictability and stock phrasing — a tool that fixes those keeps your meaning, and a tool that only swaps synonyms does not. If your institution requires disclosure of AI assistance, no humanizer changes that obligation — see our responsible use policy.

Frequently asked questions

Which is the best AI humanizer overall?

There is no single answer, because the tools optimize for different buyers. For the lowest yearly price it is Undetectable.ai at about $5 a month billed annually; for volume on a budget, Walter Writes; for testing without paying, HIX Bypass's 150 free words a month; for zero budget, a free tool like Text Humanize. Judge any of them on your own test text, not on a ranking.

Are free AI humanizers safe to use?

It depends on the tool, not the price. Check the privacy policy before pasting anything sensitive: a free tool may use your text to improve a model, keep logs, or sell nothing at all. The three that matter are where the text goes, whether it is retained, and who processes it. Free tiers of paid products (trials and daily allowances) are generally safe on data handling; completely free tools should be judged on their published policy.

Do AI humanizers actually beat Turnitin and GPTZero?

Partially, and never permanently. Independent benchmarks in late 2026 put the best tools around a 75% pass rate — a real improvement over raw AI output, but far short of a guarantee. Detectors retrain, and Turnitin has specifically targeted paraphrased content. Treat a humanizer as one step in a workflow that includes checking the result yourself and editing what still reads as machine-written.

What is the difference between a humanizer and a paraphraser?

A paraphraser rewrites to change wording while preserving meaning — its goal is a different sentence, not a particular detection outcome. A humanizer targets the statistical patterns detectors measure: sentence length variation, word predictability and stock phrasing. QuillBot is mostly a paraphraser with a humanize mode; the tools in this list built for detection are humanizers. In practice the best results combine both: humanize first, then edit like a paraphrase.
