Writing with AI, done carefully

AI writing tools are good at producing a first draft and bad at producing a finished one. The gap between the two is where most of the real work lives: editing for clarity, checking facts, adjusting tone, and making sure the result actually sounds like you. These guides cover that gap — the practical, unglamorous middle of the process.

Every guide here is written to be used, not just read. Each one ends with steps you can apply to your own draft immediately. Where a claim depends on research — for example, published error rates for AI detectors — we cite the source rather than asserting a number. Where the honest answer is “it depends”, we say so instead of inventing a rule.

Start with whichever problem you actually have:

  • Your AI draft sounds generic. Read how to make ChatGPT sound more human and the common patterns detectors and editors notice.
  • You need to edit for clarity. How to edit AI writing walks through a full pass; improving readability covers sentence-level fixes.
  • You were flagged by a detector. Start with false positives, then how detection actually works.
  • You’re writing for work or school. cover letters, professional emails, argumentative essays, and citing AI tools.

Nothing here is a substitute for your own judgement. These guides are written and reviewed by our editorial team under a published editorial policy, and we correct mistakes when readers report them — tell us if you spot one.
