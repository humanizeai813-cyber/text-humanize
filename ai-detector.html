    Context

    Why AI detection is hard

    Understanding the limits helps you use any detector — ours or anyone else’s — more responsibly.

    AI detectors try to answer a question that has no perfectly observable answer: who or what wrote this text? Unlike plagiarism checking, which compares a document against a known database, detection looks for statistical fingerprints in the writing itself. That distinction matters because the fingerprints are probabilistic, not definitive.

    The accuracy problem

    Published evaluations of commercial detectors show a wide spread of reliability. Studies have reported error rates that spike dramatically for two groups in particular: non-native English writers, whose more constrained vocabulary mirrors the predictability of model output, and highly edited or formally structured human writing, which can also look uniform. One widely cited evaluation found that classifiers built on older GPT generations failed to generalise to newer models entirely after an update, dropping toward chance performance overnight.

    That failure mode is inherent: models change, prompting styles evolve, and detectors that are not continuously retrained drift out of date. When you use any score — including ours — you are seeing a snapshot of pattern-matching against the training data the detector was built on.

    What the signals actually measure

    Most detectors, including this one, combine several cheap, interpretable features rather than relying on a single opaque model. Perplexity — roughly, how “surprised” a language model is by a word choice — is a common one: AI text tends to pick high-probability words, so it reads as low-perplexity. Burstiness, the variation in sentence structure and length, is another; human writing tends to alternate between long, complex sentences and short ones, while model output is more evenly paced.

    These are useful proxies, but they are proxies. A careful human editor working from a house style guide can produce low-perplexity, low-burstiness text. A model prompted to write in fragments can produce the opposite. The score reflects the text, not the author.

    How to use a score responsibly

    • Treat it as a prompt, not a verdict. A high score is a reason to read a passage more carefully — nothing more.
    • Look at the evidence, not just the number. Which sentences were flagged and why tells you more than the aggregate score.
    • Consider the context. Form emails, technical documentation and exam answers all have legitimate reasons to sound uniform.
    • Never act on a score alone. Academic and employment decisions require human review, drafts, and conversation.

    AI detector questions

    More answers in the full FAQ.

    How accurate is this AI detector?

    No AI detector is fully accurate, including ours. The score is a directional estimate based on writing patterns. It works best on longer passages of ordinary prose and is least reliable on short, technical or highly formal text. Treat it as a prompt to look more closely, not as a verdict.

    My own writing got a high score. Why?

    Human writing can share the features detectors look for, especially if it’s formal, formulaic or written by someone using a second language. That’s a false positive. Look at which signals were flagged; varying your sentence length and replacing generic phrases usually changes the result and improves the writing too.

    Can teachers use this to check student work?

    Only as a starting point for a conversation. Research has shown that AI detectors can misclassify writing by non-native English speakers at high rates. A score should never be the sole basis for an accusation. Talk with the student and look at drafts, notes and revision history.

    Is the text I check saved?

    The text is processed to calculate the score and is not saved to a database. If an external detection model is enabled, the text is also sent to that provider for analysis. See our privacy policy for details.

    Is this AI detector really free?

    Yes — free with no account, no subscription and no trial period. Every check costs nothing, and there is no daily or monthly word quota. The only limits are practical ones: each run accepts up to 10,000 characters (about 1,600 words), and rapid-fire automated requests are briefly paused to keep the tool fast for everyone.

    Can I check a whole document for free?

    Yes, up to 10,000 characters per run — around four double-spaced pages. For longer documents, check them section by section; that also gives you a more useful picture, because you can see which individual sections carry the strongest AI-like patterns instead of one blended score for the whole file.

    What is the most accurate free AI detector?

    No detector deserves that title outright, including this one. Published evaluations show every tool producing both false positives and misses, with accuracy swinging most for short, technical or non-native English writing. The honest approach: test two or three detectors on writing where you already know the answer — your own drafts versus obvious AI output — and judge them on how they handle that, not on marketing claims. Then read the actual signals, as our checker shows, instead of trusting a single number.
